Juice bars that sell "cold-pressed" juice often use hydraulic presses. These machines first grind produce into a pulp, then squeeze that pulp under heavy pressure through a filter bag. The result is a bright, smooth juice with almost no foam. It also keeps longer than juice from other methods.

Home hydraulic presses used to be rare and very expensive. There are now more options, including two-stage machines that combine an auger with a press. Here are five worth considering.

## Hydraulic press vs. masticating juicer

A **hydraulic press** gives you the smoothest juice with the most yield and the longest fridge life. It's also the slowest and most expensive option, and the filter bags need washing.

A **masticating juicer** gets you most of the way there for less money and less effort. If you're new to juicing, start with one of our [best masticating juicers](/best-masticating-juicers-2026/) and upgrade later if you fall in love with cold-pressed juice.

## FAQ

**How long does hydraulic-pressed juice last?** Kept cold in sealed glass bottles, home-pressed juice is commonly kept for two to three days. Commercial juice bars may use extra processing to extend shelf life, which home juice doesn't have.

**Are press bags reusable?** Yes. Rinse them right after use, wash them with unscented soap, and let them dry fully. Replace them when they start to fray.

If green juice is a daily habit for you, a masticating juicer is usually the upgrade that makes the biggest difference. Instead of shredding produce at high speed like a centrifugal machine, a masticating (or "slow") juicer crushes and presses it with a rotating auger. That gentler process handles kale, spinach and wheatgrass much better. It also creates less foam and heat, so your juice separates more slowly in the fridge.

Below are the eight slow juicers we'd look at first in 2026, followed by a short buying guide.

## How to choose a masticating juicer

**Vertical or horizontal?** Vertical juicers take up less counter space and feed more easily. Horizontal juicers are usually better with leafy greens and wheatgrass, and they often double as food processors for nut butters and sorbets.

**Chute size.** A wider chute means less chopping. If prep time is what stops you from juicing, prioritize it.

**Cleanup.** Count the parts and look at the strainer. The machine you can clean in two minutes is the one you'll actually use.

**What you juice most.** For mostly greens, choose a horizontal or twin-gear model. For fruit and mixed produce, a vertical juicer is easier.

**Warranty.** Slow juicers have long warranties because the motors work at low speed. Treat a long warranty as a quality signal.

## FAQ

**Is masticating juice healthier than centrifugal juice?** Both contain the same basic nutrients from the produce. Masticating juicers tend to extract more juice from greens and create less foam and oxidation, so the juice keeps a little better.

**How long does slow-juiced juice last?** Ideally drink it the same day. Stored in a full, airtight glass jar in the fridge, it'll usually keep for up to 48 hours.

**Do I need to peel produce?** Remove thick, bitter or inedible peels like citrus rinds and pineapple skin. Thin skins on apples, cucumbers and carrots can stay on.

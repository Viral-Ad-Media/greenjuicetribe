"Should I buy a juicer or a blender?" is the question we get most. Both turn fruit and vegetables into something you can drink, but they make very different things.

![Juicers vs blenders](/uploads/2022/10/blenders-Vs-Juicers-2.jpg)

## The core difference

- **Juicing** separates the liquid from the fiber. You drink the juice and the pulp is left behind.
- **Blending** purées the whole fruit or vegetable, so the fiber stays in your glass.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Juicing | Blending |
|---|---|---|
| Fiber | Mostly removed | Fully kept |
| Fullness | Light, less filling | More filling, can be a meal |
| Produce per glass | More (it takes several pounds for a large batch) | Less |
| Texture | Thin and smooth | Thick |
| Cleanup | More parts | Usually quicker |
| Typical cost | Juicers vary widely | Good blenders at every price |

## When juicing makes sense

Juice is a concentrated, easy way to take in more vegetables than you'd eat in one sitting, especially greens like celery, cucumber, kale and spinach. Many people enjoy a fresh green juice as a light morning drink.

Keep juices vegetable-forward. Fruit juices without fiber deliver sugar quickly, so a good rule of thumb is mostly veg with one piece of fruit for sweetness.

## When blending makes sense

Smoothies keep all the fiber, which helps you feel full and supports digestion. Add protein (yogurt, protein powder, nut butter) and healthy fat (chia, flax, avocado), and a smoothie can be a complete breakfast.
